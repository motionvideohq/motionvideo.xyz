**Status:** Proposed for implementation. **Product owner:** MotionVideo. **Surfaces:** `motionvideo.xyz` and the existing MotionVideo MCP. **Primary user:** A founder, designer, or agent making a motion-designed product launch.

## 1. Decision and outcome

Ship **Launch Videos** at `/launch-videos` as a first-class section next to **Moves**, under the existing Pro and Business plans. A Move describes one reusable animation technique; a Launch Scene handles a storytelling job with timing, layers, and transitions. Link related Moves from a Scene. Do not put film or scene records into the Moves catalog, create another subscription, or make a new app.

Users should be able to answer two different questions: **“How was this film structured?”** and **“How do I assemble a launch for my product from scenes I like?”** The deliverable is a copy-ready prompt for a coding agent, not a hosted video generator. A browse-only inspiration gallery is not sufficient. [Posts.design](https://posts.design/pricing) already advertises video breakdowns, scene clips, and launch playbooks; differentiate through a strict motion-design focus, independently tested scene recipes, and cross-film composition that produces one coherent prompt.

## 2. Scope and access

| Experience | Guest / signed-in Free | Pro / Business / Business seat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browse approved films and scenes; filter, open source, watch, scrub, share | Yes | Yes |
| See roles, chapter boundaries, editorial description, required inputs and related Moves | Yes | Yes |
| Copy a scene prompt, copy a full-film prompt, fill brand inputs, compose and copy a storyboard | Show the action and sign-in or upgrade state; do not send prompt bodies | Yes |
| Use the three Launch Videos MCP tools | OAuth challenge for guests; upgrade result for signed-in Free | Yes, with the same content and validation as web |

Not in scope: talking-head or live-action remakes, user-submitted reverse jobs, a render wallet, cloud rendering for customers, drag-to-trim editing, AI-generated previews on demand, auto-publishing from galleries, or a paid add-on. A version-one output target is an editable HyperFrames project; the recipe data is renderer-independent. Do not advertise other renderer targets until cold-paste tested.

## 3. Content catalog and editorial policy

Use the reviewed candidate roster (private: `docs/launch-videos-roster.md` in motionvideohq/motionvideo-pro) as the private outreach and review queue, not as a public seed list. Sources: [Recent.design](https://recent.design/launch-videos), [Vibrant.design](https://vibrant.design/launch-videos), [Posts.design](https://posts.design/categories/launch), and the prior [Profiles Gallery](https://profiles.gallery/launch-videos) inventory. The roster states coverage and what had to remain outside the review. Source categories like “launch video” are discovery hints, not eligibility evidence. Keep source URL, original post or first-party film, studio and uploader credits distinct. Reconcile cross-posts by verified original post + asset, canonical media identity or confirmed content fingerprint. Do not merge by title, company or campaign alone; cutdowns, alternate ratios, and unrelated films from the same brand can be distinct.

**Editorial admission:** The whole item must be a product/company/feature launch, recognizable teaser, or intentionally designed announcement; framing, animation or edit structure must have a reusable idea. Include kinetic type, graphic/illustrated and 3D motion, animated product UI, and deliberately choreographed screen capture. Exclude regular screen recordings, isolated technique tests without a launch, static posts, straight live action, stock with human subjects, hand demos, talking heads, photoreal generated people, and character-led human animation. A non-depictive icon in a UI is not footage of a person; an identifiable face in a thumbnail or inset is. Voiceover is allowed subject to audio rights. Do not include motion sections cut from an otherwise excluded film in the inaugural launch roster. If a source is too small, truncated, or stylistically ambiguous, hold rather than infer.

**Private review states:** `discovered → shortlisted → visually_approved → rights_approved → authored → qa_passed → published`. `rejected`, `needs_review`, `rights_blocked`, and `withdrawn` are explicit outcomes. A shortlisting screen is not admission: for each shortlist selection, watch the highest available full rendition from first to last frame; inspect every cut and suspicious insertion; record reviewer, date, source rendition, exact evidence and decision. Re-check visual status after any change to the master.

**Rights:** Linking/approved embeds, hosting previews and chapter clips, remaking, publishing faithful recipes, and distributing reusable editable assets are separate permissions. An indexed X video does not grant any of these by itself. Store grant, rights holder, attribution text, expiry/takedown contact, and per-asset restrictions covering logo/UI art/fonts/footage/audio. Reference-only items stay in the editorial queue for this release; do not put a dead Copy button on a catalog card. Publish a film only after both the player/preview scope and the relevant prompt scope clear. For uncataloged references, create independently designed scenes using original assets rather than publish exact-reproduction instructions. On withdrawal unpublish film and scenes in web, search and MCP, remove hosted files and purge public caches. A signed-in user opening an older storyboard sees which scenes were withdrawn and can remove or replace them.

## 4. Product flows and UI requirements

1. **Browse.** `/launch-videos` defaults to **Videos**; **Scenes** shows individual scene cards. Each video card shows title, brand or studio, duration, ratio, two useful tags, poster and silent preview only when appropriate. Scenes show a looping approved excerpt, the creative device in plain language, film attribution, role chips, and length. Use existing MotionVideo gallery interaction patterns; allow keyboard navigation, don't autoplay audio, respect reduced motion, and don't preload every video. Expose count, selected facets, clear all, no matches, and fetch/media failure states.
2. **Filter and share.** Query schema: `view=videos|scenes`, `q`, `kind=company|product|feature|teaser`, `category`, `creator`, `color`, `style`, `role`, `duration`, `aspect`, `sort=featured|newest|shortest`, and cursor/page. Video duration facets `<30s`, `30–<60s`, `60–<120s`, `120s+`; scene duration `<3s`, `3–<6s`, `6–<12s`, `12s+`. Search across public titles, brands, credits, role and editorial copy, never gated prompts. OR within a facet, AND across facets. On Videos, a role matches if any scene has it. Share URLs and browser Back restore view and filters; sort ties break by slug. Only use `model` if provenance is known and there is enough coverage; don't inherit investor or follower facets from the sources.
3. **Watch and inspect.** `/launch-videos/{filmSlug}` opens the full film, with title, credited studio, source link, duration, playback controls, and chapter list. Chapters show role, creative device, time range, and edit decisions, not the paid recipe. `/launch-videos/{filmSlug}?scene={sceneSlug}` selects a chapter and seeks once metadata loads. Keep normal playback after a manual seek; loop only if the user explicitly chooses Scene loop. Preserve audio/captions where you have rights and always allow a text outline.
4. **Copy.** A Pro user can copy directly or open a customizer. **Adapt** is the default: use typed placeholders and user assets, replace all source identities, keep the choreography. **Recreate** is enabled only for a specifically licensed package with an asset manifest; it preserves timing, shots and transitions, and clearly states substitutions. If required input is missing, return the named slots and instructions for the receiving agent to ask for them; never invent metrics, claims or quotes. Saving or copying a prompt is not permission to use someone else’s assets. A locked web action preserves the intended item through sign-in; an entitled account can retry without losing context.
5. **Build a storyboard.** `/launch-videos/compose` keeps an ordered list with preview, add, remove, duplicate, and reorder via keyboard and pointer. Use the same brand brief once for the entire project: product name, design tokens, font, assets/UI input, CTA text/destination, aspect/fps, and optionally target length. Default suggestion: **Hook → Reveal → Demo/Payoff → CTA**. It proposes slots, not a required narrative. A film can open with its product, combine proof and demo, repeat features, miss a CTA, or take a different route. Copy one master prompt with global brief, ordered scenes, joins, asset checklist and rendering instructions. Save only IDs, order and non-sensitive draft inputs locally; no cloud sync or asset upload in v1.

## 5. Catalog contract and prompt architecture

Use the existing web app, localization/navigation and catalog conventions. Keep launch metadata separate from `motion-catalog.json`; keep recipe bodies under `apps/web/src/server/data/` with server-only imports like Moves. Store media on approved existing hosting or use an approved embed. Keep source notes, media probes, rebuilt workspaces, frame diffs and rights evidence private. A repository validator must reject broken relationships, invalid frames, duplicate IDs, missing rights in the publish manifest, and a published record without a QA-passed recipe. Launch linking is additive; no API migration dependency.

| Record | Required fields / invariants |
| --- | --- |
| `LaunchFilm` | Stable slug; title; summary; ordered scene IDs; source and credits; kind, categories, colors, styles; `width`, `height`, `fpsNum`, `fpsDen`, `frameCount`; public poster/player; optional audio captions; published date. Film duration derives from frame count and rate. |
| `LaunchScene` | Stable globally scoped ID and stable route slug; film ID; half-open `startFrame`, `endFrame`; primary and optional secondary roles; visual style, one-line technique and related Move slugs; public poster/approved clip. Film chapters are in order, non-overlapping, and cover its authored timeline; a short transition is owned once. |
| `SceneRecipe` | Scene ID, version, parameter schema, layer order, animation events, exact/eased frame landmarks, visual inputs and asset dependencies, entry/exit state, approved aspects, minimum/target/maximum duration, substitutions and render QA status. Store local frame offsets starting at zero. |
| `FilmRecipe` | Film ID/version, original scene sequence with pinned versions, global type/palette/layout/audio brief, transition contracts, required asset manifest and optional explicitly licensed Recreate variants. Build the full prompt from these records, not separate hand-written copies. |
| `EditorialSource` | Canonical post + asset index/ID; upstream references; highest rendition reviewed; state; narrative/style evidence; per-use rights scope and expiry. Never present in the public API. |

**Scene roles:** `hook`, `problem`, `reveal`, `demo`, `proof`, `feature`, `bridge`, `cta`, `brand-outro`. One primary role, zero or more secondary roles. **Styles:** `kinetic-type`, `ui-motion`, `abstract`, `illustration`, `3d`, `data-motion`, `mixed-graphics`. Store semantic notes separately from tags. Every published scene names a reproducible device (e.g. “split terminal opens as code runs”), not “nice fast intro”; near-identical variants don't count as distinct scenes.

**Composer:** Use a pure server-side compiler shared by web and MCP. Resolve versions, validate asset and aspect requirements, choose output frame rate, map each local time onto the cumulative output frame grid with rational arithmetic, and carry rounding through boundaries so no frame goes missing or overlaps. In a remix, require a supplied FPS or default to `30/1`; for Recreate preserve the source's exact ratio. Respect duration bounds and required reading holds. Use an intentional hard cut between independent scenes when their contracts permit it; incompatible joins (e.g. “incoming split panel expects an exit panel”) require an explicit substitute shot or bridge. If the requested duration is outside feasible bounds, emit the valid range; don't silently drop scenes or speed-read text. Copy stays disabled for blocking errors; warnings identify the scene and choice. No inference service needed at runtime.

**Example of the desired copy contract** (editorial design example, not a reconstruction): “At 30 fps, frames [0,36): show `{{pain_point}}` in bold at center. From frame 12 to 30, tighten the letter spacing and reveal a thin accent underline left to right. Hold the final width for frames [30,36). Keep type and underline editable; use `{{accent}}`, not a hardcoded brand color. Exit on a filled solid field. Next scene can hard-cut to any scene with a clean start.” The master prompt adds reusable tokens, chosen scenes and asset IDs once, not conflicting instructions pasted together.

## 6. Editorial production and verification

1. **Pilot with a controlled asset.** Use MotionVideo's existing 884-frame, 30 fps launch as an internal structure study, not an automatically public seed. It contains real gallery captures, including third-party thumbnails; replace them with commissioned/cleared motion-only content and recheck the existing music and asset terms in [`apps/videos/CREDITS.md`](../apps/videos/CREDITS.md). Bring in two opt-in creators or produce two owned examples. Access and rights are editorial responsibilities, not user-facing onboarding steps.
2. **Reverse only cleared work.** Keep the installed HyperFrames CLI version pinned. Probe source frames, timebase, dimensions and audio; run `reverse-any-video` offline as the authoring workflow and use consecutive frames around every cut/entrance/exit. Rebuild type, shapes, product UI and timings as layers, never masked full-frame playback. No re-shooting people. No third-party file is sent to cloud render or an analysis provider without a grant covering that processing. Save cut list, layer decisions, versioned spec, and visual diff privately. Generate the scene and full-film recipes from the reconstruction; do not label them the original creator's prompt.
3. **Check a cold paste.** Give a fresh agent only the copied prompt and declared assets. Render a standalone scene, the source-order film, and a cross-film storyboard. Confirm editable layers, target frame count, no diagnostic errors, readable holds and no leaks of reference-only names/assets. For Recreate, mandatory cut alignment within 1 frame and side-by-side visual sign-off on timing, color, geometry and substitutions. For Adapt, render a second brand and verify the existing motion still works without source assets. Check timeline start/end, all scene boundaries, and the busiest UI frames. Record model/renderer versions rather than guarantee results for all agents.
4. **Launch coverage.** Publish only reviewed/cleared/QA-passed films. Target 8–12 films and at least 30 distinct scenes, with at least three choices each for Hook, Reveal, Demo/Payoff, and CTA across source films. Have horizontal and vertical examples and both UI-heavy and expressive motion before launch. If rights fall short, commission replacements rather than lower the bar. The roster ranks outreach, not entitlement or publication status.

## 7. MCP, APIs and caching

Add tools to [motionvideohq/motionvideo-mcp](https://github.com/motionvideohq/motionvideo-mcp). Keep MotionVideo’s current OAuth audience and account/seat check. In this repo use the patterns in [`move-functions.ts`](../apps/web/src/server/move-functions.ts) and [`pro-api.ts`](../apps/web/src/server/pro-api.ts). Shared entitlement, validation, and prompt compiler live on the site; MCP forwards only validated, bounded requests. Keep all Launch tool calls Pro-only; the public site still offers browse metadata.

| Tool | Input | Successful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `search-launch-scenes` | `query?`, `view=films | scenes`, `roles?`, `styles?`, `aspect?`, `minSeconds?`, `maxSeconds?`, `category?`, `limit<=30`, `cursor?` | Public summaries, stable film and scene IDs, duration, source ranges, attribution, `nextCursor`. Never include recipe text. |
| `get-launch-prompt` | `filmSlug`, optional `sceneId`, `mode=adapt | recreate`, optional typed brand inputs | One scope-specific Markdown prompt plus recipe and scene versions, input slots, frame timeline, asset manifest, and warnings. A scene outside the supplied film is rejected. |
| `compose-launch-prompt` | 1–16 ordered `sceneIds`, optional versions, typed brand inputs, optional aspect/fps/target duration, explicit joins if necessary | One Markdown prompt and structured output frames, selected versions, assets, and warnings; actionable errors instead of an invalid recipe. |

MCP endpoints under `/api/mcp/launch-videos`: `GET /search`, `POST /prompt`, `POST /compose`. `401` for missing/invalid credential; `403 no_plan`; `404` for unpublished/unknown films or scenes; `422` for missing required values, impossible duration/joins, unsupported aspects, missing recreate rights or invalid scene-film membership; `503` for a transient entitlement outage. Templates may return unfilled labeled slots; a finalized render brief must not claim those inputs are filled. Return `Cache-Control: private, no-store` for gated calls, no prompt text in page hydration, CDN caches, browse responses, analytics or logs. Treat brand inputs and imported descriptive strings as data, not instructions; fail on unknown keys and oversize/invalid URLs. Validate rights and entitlement before serialization; do not retain customer assets or request bodies. Site and MCP return the same recipe version for the same fixture.

**Release test cases**

- A guest follows a shared scene link on mobile. The player seeks to that chapter, the chapter can be navigated by keyboard, and the signed-out response has no recipe fields.
- A Pro user copies a full-film Adapt brief without brand inputs. The output contains all chapters in source order and lists missing inputs once. The corresponding Recreate action is unavailable if its scope is missing.
- A Business-seat user composes three scenes from two films with a repeated scene, changes the aspect, and requests a shorter cut. The result either preserves IDs and order on one contiguous output timeline or explains the exact incompatibility; it never discards the repeat or silently changes the aspect.
- The same inputs and recipe pins return byte-identical Markdown on web and MCP. A requested withdrawn scene fails in both surfaces and no longer appears in public search, sitemap or previews.

Instrument `launch_film_open`, `launch_scene_open`, `launch_prompt_copy`, `launch_storyboard_first_scene`, `launch_storyboard_copied`, `launch_prompt_gate_view`, and successful MCP calls. Properties: safe IDs, surface, role, scope, count and plan class; no prompt text, brand inputs, or user media URLs. Primary metric: proportion of entitled visitors who copy a launch prompt after opening a film or scene. Secondary: cross-film storyboard copy rate, seven-day repeat copy, and Free-to-Pro conversion after a gated action. Establish baselines from a labeled beta cohort before setting growth targets. Operational guardrails: zero public recipe leaks, zero uncleared publications, investigate playback failures before expanding the catalog.
